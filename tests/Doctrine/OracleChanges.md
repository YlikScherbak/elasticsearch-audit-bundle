# Changes to the oracle of the search

`WhatTheHistorySaysAgainstWhatTheRowsDidTest` holds the history to what the rows did. Its
oracle reads the database and never the bundle: a change to it is a change to what the
bundle is held to, and one made to fit the code is how a search goes blind. So every change
is listed here with the decision that asked for it. A change that cannot name one is not a
change to make.

## Fixes to the runner of the scenarios

What is listed here changes how the generator plays the application, not what the oracle holds the
history to: no expectation moved. They are listed apart so that a corpus that changed under one of
them is not read as a new rule.

| Commit  | What changed in the runner | Why |
|---------|----------------------------|-----|
| (removals, ORM 2.19) | After a step, a manager the step left closed is opened again on the same connection with the same listeners (`reopen()`), as an application does after a flush it could not complete (`ManagerRegistry::resetManager()`). And the world is the one the rows have: the article or a tag whose row is gone is gone from it, asked past the connection (`rowIsThere()`), whatever Doctrine still holds. | Both reviewers (2026-09-30). ORM 2.19 fails the flush of a collection it re-inserts a join row of (a unique constraint), and the runner went on with a closed manager; and after a postFlush listener threw, Doctrine -- ORM 3 too -- keeps an entity it removed in the identity map, and a step that tagged it wrote a join row to a row that is gone, which the database refused. The corpus of the ORM 2.19 image ran to its end for the first time: 3000 of 3000, no document changed between bb0460a and 4c62cc9. On ORM 3, every vocabulary, 15000 sequences: one changed, seed 1107. Its steps are 'remove a tag, flush, publishing swallowed, add a namesake, edit the kiln's root, flush, and after the statement a nested one edits the kiln's subclass, remove a line, flush, edit the kiln's subclass, edit the article, flush, and after the statement a nested one tags the article, flush'. The runner before tagged the article with the tag the swallowed flush had removed; the application's flush was refused (ROLLBACK TO SAVEPOINT), and the sequence ended with five records. The runner now leaves that tag out, the flush completes, and the sequence has eight records: the article's title, the kiln's heat and the article's new tag besides. The oracle agrees with both. The price: the ORM 2.19 corpus no longer edits an article it removed, the road of the UPDATE that reaches no row (ba599b1), which AnUpdateThatReachedNoRowTest holds instead. |
