# Changes to the oracle of the search

`WhatTheHistorySaysAgainstWhatTheRowsDidTest` holds the history to what the rows did. Its
oracle reads the database and never the bundle: a change to it is a change to what the
bundle is held to, and one made to fit the code is how a search goes blind. So every change
is listed here with the decision that asked for it. A change that cannot name one is not a
change to make.

| Commit  | What changed in the oracle | The decision it follows |
|---------|----------------------------|-------------------------|
| 3f670e2 | Readings taken around a flush nested in another, not only around a step (`checkpoints`). | Decision A (2026-09-23): the history describes each flush, not the operation. |
| 0ebdf90 | A line waiting for its INSERT is not a phantom (`holdsAPhantom()`), asked of the unit of work and not of the id. | None needed: an assumption of the generator's (id means row), wrong where a sequence hands out ids; the same seeds now draw the same sequences everywhere. |
| 22954c8 | Readings before every statement that writes, on the connection that runs it, and the readings of what a rollback undid dropped. | UPDATE then DELETE of one row in one flush is two facts (2026-09-24, both reviewers): the oracle saw only the edges of flushes and netted such a pair. |
| cdb06b2 | A line moved and changed by one statement: the change is said about the crate it arrived at. | The move rule (2026-09-24, both reviewers and the owner; UPGRADE 1.3): the change goes with the arrival, its old side the line's value. Eight of 3000 sequences, kept in TELLS_APART. |
| (step 4, vocabulary) | A change of a line's quantity is not said when the row has no crate after it, moved or not: the branch for a line that did not move said it under crate "", which is no crate. | The rule that a change inside a line no crate owns belongs to no crate (`testAChangeInsideALineWhoseRowHasNoOwnerBelongsToNoCrate`), held for a move to nowhere since cdb06b2 and now for a line that stays nowhere. Reached first by the widened vocabulary: after a refused move back, Doctrine believes a loose line is in the crate, and 'change a line' changes its quantity alone. Two of 3000 sequences, both that shape. |
| (5.2c, generator) | The oracle reads the tables of a JOINED entity and of relays -- a kiln as one entity in two tables (`label`, `site`, `firing`, `heat`), a relay's reference said by the name of the relay it points at, with the world's relay names refused when two are alike -- and says what the history says of them in the same form. The corpora of 3.3 and 5.2 read the tables they always read. | Both reviewers (2026-09-27): the search had no hierarchy and no cyclic reference, so three conditions of the reader survived it without meaning anything; widened before 5.3, the old corpora kept under their own vocabularies. |
| (5.2c, generator) | An audited entity's creation and removal are statements of the oracle: its row appearing or going between two readings -- taken before every statement, so a row created and removed in one operation is both -- against the history's `create` and `remove` records. And relays created pointing at each other and never pointed elsewhere, by the sequence's own steps, have no `update` record. | Both reviewers (2026-09-27): the values alone could not see an event -- a creation's completion split into a record of its own left them the same. The relays' expectation is the scenario's, not a rule of the reader's. |
| (5.2c, generator) | A fact is its value and who wrote it: what the rows did is signed by the actor of the flush running the statement -- this test's own observer of flushes, taking who was acting at each onFlush and the connection's nesting level, the innermost flush running at a shallower level than the statement being its owner; never the bundle's log -- and what the history says is signed by the record's source. In step 5's world a sequence runs as one of two actors by its seed, and a flush nested in another as a third. With savepoints only: without them the statement taken back inside a flush makes the transaction rollback-only and the manager closes under the rest of the sequence, so `outer` is held by WhoWroteItTest and WhoseMomentALateRecordCarriesTest in both modes. | Both reviewers (2026-09-27): a corpus of one actor could not tell authorship apart, and three hand-written tests were its only guard. Compared with the value, not as a separate multiset of authors, so that two authors swapped between two changes are seen. |
| (5.2c, generator) | What step 5's world was widened for is counted as reached, not drawn -- a kiln changed in a nested flush, taken back, or in both tables in one execution; a relay written and taken back, or in a nested flush; a flush nested under another actor writing a row the history is about; a nested flush carrying out the outer flush's plan -- and held to the words' floors. A new ending, the README's recipe for an application's own transaction around a flush rolled back in an atomic frame, is the road to a relay's INSERT being taken back. | Both reviewers (2026-09-27): the combinations are what the widening is for, and a world that stops reaching one has lost its shape whatever its words do. |
